## 3. EPF Scheme 2026 — verified points

1. The 2026 Scheme is made under section 15(1)(a) of the Code on Social Security, 2020 and supersedes the EPF Scheme, 1952, subject to the saving for things done or omitted before supersession.
2. Paragraph 18 provides for employer contribution at 12% of wages and an equal employee contribution; 10% applies to specified notified classes. Contributions are subject to the notified wage ceiling.
3. Paragraph 46 governs partial withdrawals. The minimum balance to be retained is 25% of the aggregate contributions (employee and employer shares, together with interest) in the Provident Fund account. The specific grounds, waiting periods, caps and frequency limits must be taught from the relevant sub-paragraph, not compressed into a blanket claim that every withdrawal permits 100% of the account.
4. The scheme contains current International Worker provisions; the old Para 83 of the 1952 Scheme should not be presented as the operative 2026 rule.
5. The Code's notified EPF wage ceiling is ₹25,000 per month from 17 September 2026 (S.O. 5109(E)); it is not the EPS ceiling.

## 4. EPS Scheme 2026 — verified points

1. G.S.R. 527(E) supersedes the Employees’ Pension Scheme, 1995 and Employees’ Family Pension Scheme, 1971.
2. Paragraph 4(1): 8.33% of wages up to the notified wage ceiling is remitted by the employer to the Pension Fund.
3. Paragraph 4(2), as amended by G.S.R. 890(E), provides the Central Government contribution of 1.16% on wages up to ₹15,000 per month. The amendment is stated to take effect from 17 September 2026.
4. G.S.R. 847(E) inserts a further membership category into paragraph 7(1): a person who became an EPF Scheme 2026 member but not an EPS member, whose wages on the date of notification of the new wage ceiling were at or below the notified ceiling. This should be taught as a specific transition provision; do not simplify it into a blanket rule for every employee above/below a ceiling.
5. Paragraph 9: eligible service is determined from contributory service; the rounding rule treats six months or more as a year and less than six months as ignored (subject to the scheme's specific existing-member and seasonal-worker provisions).
6. Paragraph 10: where a member superannuates after at least 20 years of pensionable service, two years' weightage is added.
7. Paragraph 11: pensionable wages are based on the average monthly wages during the 60 months immediately preceding exit, subject to the wage ceiling applicable to each wage-ceiling period.
8. Paragraph 12: ordinarily, at least 10 years' eligible service is required for superannuation or early pension. The formula for a member other than an existing member is pensionable wages × pensionable service ÷ 70. Early pension may begin no earlier than age 50, with a 4% reduction for each year short of superannuation age. The scheme states a ₹1,000 monthly minimum, subject to its express deductions/provisos.
9. The 2026 Scheme contains separate provisions for International Workers and social-security-agreement totalisation. Do not recycle the old Para 83 question as a current-law question.

## 5. EDLI Scheme 2026 — verified points

1. G.S.R. 526(E) supersedes the EDLI Scheme, 1976.
2. Paragraph 5(2) says the rate of employer contribution is to be notified by the Central Government in consultation with the Central Board, taking actuarial valuation into account. S.O. 3581(E) notifies the rate at 0.5% of wages, subject to the applicable wage ceiling. The employee does not pay an EDLI contribution.
3. Paragraph 21(1): the assurance benefit linked to average PF balance is calculated as the average balance over the preceding 12 months (or membership period if shorter); where the average exceeds ₹50,000, the amount is ₹50,000 plus 40% of the excess, subject to a ₹1,00,000 ceiling.
4. Paragraph 21(2): a minimum assurance benefit of ₹50,000 applies in the stated circumstances where the average PF balance is below ₹50,000.
5. Paragraph 21(4): the alternative wage-linked formula uses average monthly wages (subject to the notified wage ceiling) for the 12 months preceding death × 35, plus 50% of the average PF balance subject to a ₹1,75,000 ceiling; the benefit is not less than ₹2.5 lakh and not more than ₹7 lakh. The higher of the amounts under sub-paragraphs (1) and (3) is payable.
6. Paragraph 21(5) further increases the benefit by 20% in addition to the benefit admissible under sub-paragraph (1) or (3). Teach the precise sub-paragraph and formula rather than a bare “₹2.5–7 lakh” fact.
7. Paragraph 21(6) provides for the stated death-in-service situation within six months of the last contribution while the employee remains on the employer's rolls.
8. Paragraph 23(3): complete claims are to be settled and paid within 20 days. Paragraph 23(5) provides for possible 12% annual penal interest for unjustified delay beyond the period.

## 6. Corrected wage and contribution comparison

| Item | Current position to teach | Important qualification |
|---|---|---|
| EPF wage ceiling | ₹25,000/month | S.O. 5109(E), effective 17 September 2026 |
| EPS wage ceiling for Central Government contribution | ₹15,000/month | G.S.R. 890(E), amending EPS 2026 para 4(2) |
| Employer contribution to EPF | 12% | EPF Scheme 2026 para 18; subject to notified ceiling and specified exceptions |
| Employee contribution to EPF | Equal to employer contribution | EPF Scheme 2026 para 18 |
| Employer share to EPS | 8.33% | EPS Scheme 2026 para 4(1); up to applicable ceiling |
| Central Government share to EPS | 1.16% | EPS Scheme 2026 para 4(2), as amended; wages up to ₹15,000 |
| Employer contribution to EDLI | 0.5% | S.O. 3581(E); subject to applicable wage ceiling |
| Employee contribution to EPS/EDLI | Nil | Employee contributes to EPF; EPS/EDLI streams are not employee deductions |

**Caution:** Do not automatically state the old 3.67% “net EPF” employer share or the old ₹15,000 EPF ceiling as the current 2026 rule. Any split of the employer's 12% must be calculated using the applicable current ceilings, scheme rules and notifications.

## 8. PYQ provenance audit

The existing portal has eight questions labelled “PYQs”. The supplied HTML does not provide, for any of them, a verifiable combination of:
- examination name and year;
- paper/set/series;
- question number;
- original question-paper source;
- official answer key or final answer-key reference.

To authenticate them, the project owner must supply or identify the original paper(s) and answer keys. Do not infer provenance from wording or from third-party coaching compilations.

## 9. MCQ and flashcard correction decisions

- MCQ on CBT composition: retain as a legacy Act/Code transition question only after wording explicitly identifies the relevant instrument and section.
- MCQ on 25% appeal deposit: correct to section 23(3), and identify the Tribunal under section 23(1); avoid “Industrial Tribunal” unless a separate legal source establishes that identity.
- MCQ on exemption: explain that exemption under the legacy section 17 and exemption under Code section 143 are not interchangeable labels.
- MCQ on 50% wage rule: refer to Code section 2(88) and use the statutory “wages” definition carefully; do not imply all allowances are automatically excluded.
- International Worker MCQ: replace old Para 83 reference with current EPF/EPS Scheme 2026 provisions, and ask only what the verified provisions support.
- Flashcards: update the co-operative, separate PF account, wage ceiling, contributions, appeal, partial-withdrawal and International Worker cards; tag legacy-only facts clearly.
